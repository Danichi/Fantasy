# The Fifth Summon: the story guide (v2)

Written 2026-11-26, after the White Mountains, the Verdant Elves, the island
expeditions, the explorable Dark, the field stories, the traversal toolkit and
the technique library all landed. It replaces the "Next" section of
[main-story.md](main-story.md) and turns the two planning talks (the open
world, and who the Dawn really is) into one plan: the canon, the rules every
story change follows, a walkthrough from the wheat to the final fight, and the
world changes that make it hang together.

`main-story.md` still describes what is built in Acts I to III. This guide
says where it goes and how the new regions plug in.

| Verdant Elves | Six chapters: the marker, the token, the Council, the Blighted Elder, Starfall, the Star Chart | Sylwen: "the roots descend beneath the White Mountains; the broken wheel's other half sleeps beyond the desert" |

| The explorable Dark | "Use the Dawn" at any Sunwheel; a tether timer; seven ancient battle scars with memory stones; emerge at other wheels | Pure exploration; no story hook yet beyond the Gravewood echo |
| Field stories | 28 stops; a world at war; Dark rifts as a separate danger; powers are not nations | Local relief only |
| Golden Expanse (Daniel's) | The Outcast Sands: Queen Neferah, Ishara, the Great Well; purge or rising | Ishara's parting gift "you would know what it means" |

**What's wrong with it as a story**

1. **The main story is still one line.** Every chapter `requires` the one
   before it. The open-world plan's phases 1 and 2 (the Writ, the threads)
   were never built, so the mountains, the woods and the islands sit beside
   the main quest instead of inside it.
2. **Every region ends by pointing somewhere else** (the elves point at the
   mountains and the desert, the dwarves point at the Deep Hold) with no
   shared thing to collect or decide. There's no spine joining them.
3. **The twist can leak.** A player who takes the Iron Kettle at level 8 hears
   Hrafna name the Lamplighter King before they've reached the Academy. That's
   fine on its own, but nothing yet controls how much each region says, so
   by the third region the twist could be obvious.
4. **The Dark is entered through the Dawn.** "Use the Dawn · enter the Dark",
   and "the Dawn holds your outline steady" on a memory stone. That's a
   lovely clue, but it has to flip after the reveal or Act V breaks (the
   Dark is meant to be the one place he can't see you).
5. **Name collisions:**
   - "Lamplighter" is both a Lost Lantern rank and the villain's title;
   - "the Queen" in the field-story text (holding the deep crossings
     against the Demon Lord) could be the Drowned Queen or Neferah;
   - the desert is "the Sunspire Sultanate" in the field stories but "the
     Sun Throne" and "Queen Neferah" in the Outcast Sands.

This guide fixes all five.

## 2. The canon (locked)

These are the defaults from the Dawn talk. They become canon unless you
change them. Everything later in this guide depends on them.

**The Dawn is Aurelian Vaste, the Sunwright.** History calls him the
**Lamplighter King**. "The Dawn" is the name the Temple gave him centuries
later. The Temple, except for its inner circle, has no idea who it prays to.

**What he's like before the reveal:** warm, patient, faintly amused. He never
lies outright; he frames. He answers questions with better questions. His
gifts are always useful and always come with a string. He treats you as his
favourite student, and he may mean it. He should be someone players like
having in their head, so the betrayal costs them something.

**He has a case.** The Dark Century fed people. Crops grew wild, nobody
starved, the night didn't kill. He'll say so, and he won't be entirely
wrong. A tyrant with a point hurts more than a liar.

**The secret history**

| When | What happened |
|---|---|
| About 3,000 years ago | Aurelian, a scholar-sorcerer, cuts the first Sunwheel: a lens onto the world's underside, the Dark. He learns to draw its power up through the wheels. |
| The Dark Century | He makes himself king of Cresha. Waystones spread and pump the Dark's power up, so night never truly falls. The holds burn their mountains hollow to feed his light (Hrafna). The old forest burns under a sun that never sets (the elves). The southern farmland dries into what is now the Golden Expanse. The starved Dark sends things up through the cracks to feed: that's why people call it the Dark Century, though he called it the Golden Age. |
| The end of it | A knight, **Halloran**, and his knights war against him. The seven ancient scars in the Dark are where their battles struck the world's shadow. Halloran cuts the spokes from the great wheel under the capital and binds Aurelian inside it. The seal is powered from the Dark's side, so Halloran goes down and takes the empty throne to hold the lock shut with his own life. **Halloran is the Hollow King.** |
| Since then | Aurelian can't leave the wheel, so he reaches through every Sunwheel still standing. Over centuries he grows a faith around himself: the Temple of the Dawn. Its prayers and shrines are his strength. Halloran's knights hung **Wardlights** along the roads to blind him; the Temple later claimed them as its own Lost Lanterns. |
| 80 years ago | The Hollow King starts to die. Aurelian plants the idea of the Accord in the other powers' courts, because a Game means champions converging on the Hollow Throne. |
| The five ages | He summons a champion each age. The first four died because each began to learn the truth, and he took his light back. |

**Why the Dark matters to everyone.** The Dark is the world's shadow. All
magic rises from it. Whoever sits the Hollow Throne decides where that power
flows: which lands are fertile, which magic works, where the border is thin
(so where monsters come up), and where the dead go. Each power wants the
throne for its own reason: the Drowned Queen wants the sea to rise, the
Dragon King wants never to die, the Verdant Mother wants the old woods back,
the Iron Below wants revenge on the Lamplighter King. **For Aurelian the
throne is also the lock.** Kill the Hollow King and the seal goes slack. Put
a willing hand on the throne and it opens.

**Why he needs you.** Halloran's seal forbids him to command or harm anyone
born in Cresha, so he summons from other worlds. He can only reach where a
Sunwheel stands, which is why he's "the weakest of the seven" and drops you
untaught in the wheat.

**The cast, reframed**

- **The Hollow King (Halloran):** the hero who sealed him, tired and dying.
  The seal binds him too: he can't say the name, only warn. "Don't open the
  door for him."
- **The four dead champions:** Elin found out and wrote it down. Oswald went
  to the dwarves and learned of the Lamplighter King. Hallam refused the Dawn
  at Harrow Ford and had his light pulled out of him mid-battle. Tobin ran
  into the Dark to hide and the Dark kept him (the Rust Knight).
- **Malric, the Wheelbreaker:** the Iron Below's champion, smashing wheels
  for twenty years because every wheel is one of the old king's eyes. Cruel
  to you because you're the key. A reluctant ally at the end.
- **High Priestess Seraphine** leads the **Lamplighters**, the Temple's inner
  circle, who know exactly who the Dawn is. They free him in Act VI.
- **Brother Aldous** is devout and innocent. The truth breaks him, and he
  joins you.
- **Ysra the Undying** (First Age, Verdant Mother) has watched four Dawn
  champions die, and helps you in ways you only understand later.
- **The Wheel-Shield** (First Age): his patron is the one thing this guide
  leaves open (section 8).

**Powers are not nations.** The field-story rule stays canon: the great
powers have sanctuaries, worshippers and champions, but every nation is
mortal and independent. Cresha is its own crown. The holds choose their own
alliances. An anvil badge doesn't make a dwarf the Iron Below's soldier. The
wars on the roads are mortal wars. The Dark's rifts are a separate danger
that crosses every border.

## 3. The rules for writing the story

These are the guidelines every new chapter, region or line of dialogue
follows.

### 3.1 The shape

1. **One gate: finishing Act I.** After the Night of Bells, Maud gives you a
   Wayfarer's Writ and the road is yours. Nothing else is locked behind the
   main story.
2. **Threads, not a line.** After Act I the story is a set of threads, one per
   region and power. Each thread gives the spine exactly three things:
   - **a spoke** (counts toward the Turning);
   - **a truth** (counts toward Doubt, section 3.3);
   - **an ally** (decides who stands with you in Acts V and VI).
3. **Every thread ends in a conversation that names at least two other
   threads.** "The roots descend beneath the mountains" is the model. A player
   should never finish a region and wonder where to go.
4. **The Turning opens at five spokes from any threads.** Act IV opens after
   the Turning plus any three finished threads. More threads never gate
   anything, they only add allies and change scenes.
5. **Chapters open from what you know, not what you finished.** A chapter's
   `requires` becomes a knowledge flag ("you've heard of black iron", "you
   carry the Star Chart"). You pick knowledge up from people, books and
   places, in any order.

### 3.2 The clue ladder (protecting the twist)

Truths come in three rungs. **A region may give rungs 1 and 2 at any time.
Rung 3 waits for the throne.**

| Rung | What it says | Examples | Who may say it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Something is off | The Dawn is strange, or there's a forgotten history | The Dawn knows where you'll land; "the Dawn holds your outline steady"; the Vigil's unsung verse; a mural with its face chiselled off | anyone, anywhere |
| 2. There was a Lamplighter King | He existed, he cut the wheels, he ruled through them, he was sealed | Hrafna's history; Elin's pages; the Star Chart's missing century; the Dark's memory stones | regional lorekeepers, books, the Dark |
| 3. The Dawn is him | The link itself | the Hollow King's memory flooding into you | **nobody before the throne** |

Rules:

- **Nobody alive says rung 3 before Act IV.** Characters may *ask* the
  question (Durin: "ask yourself why it never mentions any of this"). They
  never answer it. The player can suspect; the game never confirms.
- **The Dawn always has an answer.** Every rung-2 truth has a Dawn reply that
  sounds right. Bring Hrafna's history up at a wheel and he says: "A tyrant
  called Lamplighter? Yes. The dwarves have hated the light for three
  thousand years. I don't blame them. I was the one who freed them from him."
  It's true from a certain point of view, and it's exactly what he'd say.
- **The player always has a reason to keep going** even when they suspect: if
  nobody reaches the throne, the Drowned Queen's champion will, and she
  means to drown the coast.
- **Plant for the second playthrough.** A clue should read as flavour the
  first time and as a confession the second.

### 3.3 Doubt

A hidden count of rung-2 truths gathered (most threads give one, the Dark's
memories give more). It never changes what happens, only how it reads:

- **Low Doubt:** the reveal is a shock. The Hollow King's memory plays in
  full.
- **High Doubt:** you already half-know. At the throne you can say "I know
  what you are" before the Dawn asks you to sit, and his mask drops a beat
  earlier.
- **In between:** the Dawn gets a little less patient with each truth you
  raise at a wheel.

### 3.4 Order shapes the story

A `chronicle` records the order you finished threads in. Lines, champion
encounters and cutscenes read from it. Write at least one line per thread
that changes depending on what came before:

- **Mountains before the capital:** Malric opens the duel with "You've walked
  my master's halls."
- **The Star Chart before Hrafna:** Hrafna sees it and stops mid-sentence:
  "Then the elves kept a sky with a hole in it too."
- **The Sunken Isles before the Four:** Oswald's tomb has drowned-glass on
  it, and you know what that means.

### 3.5 Fair wherever you go

- **Levels by distance from where you arrive** (already built for the
  mountains: 6 to 18 at the cove, 16 to 30 up the road, 30 to 45 beyond).
  Every region gets the same treatment: a gentle edge, a dangerous heart.
- **Story fights scale inside their chapter's range.** Field bosses, lairs
  and sanctuary bosses keep fixed levels for players who want a challenge.
- **Soft gates, not walls:** cold (mountains), heat (desert), the tether
  (the Dark), getting lost (the woods), ship size (the open sea). Each one
  can be handled several ways: gear, food, a calling, a class, an origin.
- **Honest warnings:** the region card shows recommended levels and your
  level is coloured against them.

### 3.6 Voice

- Plain words. If a line needs the lore doc to make sense, rewrite it.
- No line refers to the player's gender. Use "you", "sky-born" or "them".
- Every region keeps its own speech (the dwarves blunt, the elves careful,
  the Sun Throne courtly and cruel).
- In-game text uses hyphens, never em-dashes.

### 3.7 Systems flip after the reveal

The second half shouldn't play like the first. These are the flips, written
down now so earlier content can plant them:

| System | Before Act IV | After Act IV |
|---|---|---|
| Sunwheels | Fast travel, rest, rites | His eyes. Using one reveals you; Lamplighter hunters arrive. Breaking one is a mission. |
| The Dark | Entered by "Use the Dawn"; he holds your tether | Your refuge. Entered through relit Wardlights, the ridge passage and rifts; the tether comes from Halloran's crown, not from him |
| Lost Lanterns | A collection, a chapel turn-in, the "Lamplighter" rank | Wardlights: safe houses he can't see. The rank renames itself |
| The Temple and its shrines | Healers, blessings, the Dawn Vigil | Seraphine's Lamplighters; shrines are targets |
| The day | Normal day and night | Act VI: the night stops falling over Cresha |
| Frostpeak's hood | A promise to Durin | If you kept it, Frostpeak is your one safe city above ground |

## 4. The walkthrough

### Act I: The Fifth Summon (Elder Glen). Built.

Unchanged, with three small edits:

1. **The Dawn sounds a touch too pleased** when you kill things. One line
   after Grukk, one after the Night of Bells.
2. **The Hollow King's "Don't open the door for him"** goes into the Gravewood
   scene (rung 1: "him" is never explained).
3. **The Night of Bells ends with the Writ.** Maud: "The Glen's yours to
   leave now. Here: anyone on the King's Road will know Elder Glen vouches for
   you." The Road to Port Aurelle becomes one lead among several, not the only
   door.

### The Wayfarer's Road (the open middle)

When you leave the Glen, the Journal's **Threads** page opens with three
leads already on it: the Academy (Maud), the woods (Nimri at the marker
stone, a short walk north), and the sea (Port Aurelle's harbour). Every
other thread is found by going places and talking to people.

**The threads**

The Vigil's unsung verse names a Lamplighter (1) | the King; Aldous | built; the Crown rider to add |

**The Dark runs through all of them.** It's not a thread. It's the
connective tissue:

- The seven ancient scars become **Halloran's war, told in seven memories**.
  Each memory stone already gives tether time. Rewrite their text so each one
  is a moment of that war (section 5.4). All seven together give a rung-2
  truth and a large Doubt step.
- Each region's Dark-side counterpart wheel shows what that region looked
  like in the Dark Century (a mountain burnt hollow, a forest of glass-ash,
  a dry basin where the Expanse is now).
- The Gravewood echo, the crypt and the standing-stone vision stay as they
  are.

**The Turning** (built, reframed): five spokes from any threads. Malric tries
to stop you setting them: "Every spoke you set, he gets a hand free." The
Wheel-Shield comes through the Dark. You last until Ysra steps in.

**Spare spokes.** There are now more than five spokes in the world (Malric's
box, the Four, the Deep Hold, the World Tree, the trenches, perhaps Ishara's
gift). The Turning takes five. After it, Malric offers to break any you
didn't set. Each spoke he breaks takes one power away from the Dawn's final
fight. Each spoke you set beyond five gives the Dawn one more. You're never
told this outright; Malric only says "Give me the rest. Trust me or don't."

### Act III: The Hollow Road (to build)

The Turning doesn't just turn the oldest wheel. It opens a road to the Hollow
Throne, deep in the Dark beyond the mirror of the capital. This act is where
the explorable Dark becomes the main playspace.

- **The Deep Dark:** a new band of the Dark below the current ancient
  continent, reached only from the turned wheel. The tether rules still hold.
  The Dawn gives you a long tether for this trip (he wants you there), which
  is itself a clue.
- **The race:** every power's champion is heading for the throne. Three set
  pieces:
  1. **The bridge.** Hold a span of black stone against the Drowned Queen's
     champion. If you made a deal with her in the Drowned thread, she lets
     you pass and asks you to remember it.
  2. **The shadow city.** A city of shadow-people under the capital, who
     remember Halloran as a good king and leave lanterns for him. They won't
     say his name either. They give you his crown, "for when you need to see
     in the dark" (this becomes the post-reveal tether).
  3. **Malric's last stand.** He blocks the last stair. You beat him (you've
     grown). He lets you pass with a warning you don't understand yet: "When
     it tells you to sit, don't."
- **The Lamplighter King's ruined palace** sits in the Dark's capital, a
  mirror of the one above, its murals all with the same chiselled face.

### Act IV: The Hollow Throne (to build)

- The Hollow King is a husk on a throne of black iron and old light. He
  doesn't fight to win. He fights to make you wait, and his attacks are his
  memories (the seven scars, replayed).
- The Dawn, gentle and urgent: "End it. It's mercy. If you don't, she will."
- **You choose.** Strike him, or lower your blade. If you lower it, Halloran
  asks you to end it himself ("I've held the door long enough. Let me rest.
  Just don't open it.") or, if you refuse even that, the Drowned Queen's
  champion arrives and does it. Either way he dies. The player keeps their
  dignity.
- As he dies, his memory floods into you: the Dark Century, his name, the
  seal, Aurelian's face. Rung 3.
- **The Dawn's voice, warmly: "Now sit."** You refuse (at high Doubt you
  refuse before he asks).
- For the first time Aurelian appears, a projection of pale gold through the
  throne's broken wheel: skin lit from inside like a lantern through paper,
  a slow-turning Sunwheel halo, eyes like a night sky.
- **The first fight with him.** You can't win. It ends when you throw
  yourself into the deep Dark, the one place his light can't follow. Your
  Sunwheel brand goes cold.

### Act V: The Hidden War (to build)

- **The Dark is how you travel.** Halloran's crown gives you your tether now.
  You strike up into the living world to break Sunwheels and the Dawn's
  shrines: roadside shrines, the Chapel of the Dawn at Millbrook, Temple
  chapels, the Sunwheel stones.
- **Every waystone you attuned in the first half is now a liability.** Using
  one shows you to him and Lamplighter hunters come. Players who hoarded fast
  travel feel it most.
- **Wardlights are your safe houses.** Every Lost Lantern you relit is a
  place he can't see. The players who collected them have the easiest war.
- **Frostpeak pays off.** If you left Durin's wheel hooded, Frostpeak is the
  one city above ground he can't watch. If you woke it, Durin hoods it again
  and gives you a look.
- **Allies gather by thread:** Malric, Aldous (broken by the truth, then
  steadfast), Mell and Orren, Durin, the elves, the Drowned Queen's champion
  if you dealt with her, Ishara or Neferah depending on the Sands.
- **The other powers take sides.** The Accord collapses. Some champions join
  you. One bargains with him. The Drowned Queen's champion tries to take the
  empty Hollow Throne while everyone's distracted.

### Act VI: The Sunwright Returns (to build)

- You break the great shrine under the capital. **It still costs him:** every
  shrine and spare spoke you broke in Act V takes a power away from him, so he
  comes back incomplete. It's a win that costs you, not a failure.
- Seraphine's Lamplighters free him anyway. He takes the capital and the
  throne.
- **The night stops falling over Cresha.** Crops grow wild, the heat climbs,
  festivals turn strange (the Dawn Vigil becomes a coronation). NPCs who
  loved the Dawn are happy, and that's the horror of it.
- You're on the run while he hunts you. Cresha's towns change hands one by
  one; the ones with relit Wardlights hold out longest.

### The finale

Face to face with him in the gold capital. Halloran's sword, carried since
the throne, is the only thing that wounds him. The fight's phases are built
from what you did:

- each power you denied him (spokes broken, shrines broken) is a phase he
  doesn't have;
- each ally you earned fights beside you in one phase;
- at high Doubt he stops pretending sooner, and talks to you as an equal.

Afterwards: who sits the Hollow Throne is the last choice (nobody, an ally,
you). That's the ending's variable.

Small, concrete changes to built content, so it fits the spine.

  fifth summon broke the old pact"): too close to rung 3, and "broke the old

### 5.3 Hooks into new content

- **Field stories:** Dark rifts are the border thinning as Halloran dies.
  Add one line per region to the rift outcome text saying so.
- **The Frostpeak hood:** save the choice as a story flag now (it already is:
  `frostpeakUnhooded`), so Act V can read it.

### 5.4 The seven memories (rewrite of `src/world/dark/land.ts`)

The scars stay where they are; only the words change. Each becomes a moment
of Halloran's war, told without names:

1. **The fused bowl:** "Something larger than a keep struck here. It was
   light, falling the wrong way: down, into the shadow, to burn what lived
   there."
2. **The parted ridge:** "A knight cut this ridge to stop an army that
   didn't sleep. It marched around."
3. **The black basin** (under Aurelle): "The sea pulled back from here for a
   hundred years. The things that crawled out of it were starving."
4. **The recoiling forest:** "The trees bent away from a light that never
   set, until they turned to stone rather than face it."
5. **The old foundations:** "A city stood here that kept its lamps lit for
   someone else's king. Its people left lanterns at the edge for the knights
   who came to put them out."

## 6. Three example playthroughs

How the same story reads in different orders.

**The scholar's road (closest to today).** Glen, then the Academy, the
capital, the Four, the Turning. The player meets Malric three times as an
enemy before learning why he breaks wheels. Doubt stays low until they go
north. The reveal is a shock.

**The sailor's road.** Glen, then straight to Port Aurelle's harbour, the
Iron Kettle at level 8, the lower holds, Frostpeak. The player hears the
Lamplighter King's name before they've set foot in the Academy. When Mell
reads them the index, they're already doing the sums. Malric's duel on the
plaza opens with "You've walked my master's halls." The reveal is a
confirmation.

**The wanderer's road.** Glen, the woods, the Star Chart, then the isles, then
the mountains, with Act II last. They gather spokes from the Tree, the
trenches and the Deep Hold, and reach the Turning holding more spokes than it
needs. Malric asks for the rest. Whether they trust him decides how hard the
last fight is.
